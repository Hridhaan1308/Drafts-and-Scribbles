[[Plato]] provides three proofs that the philosophic life is the happiest.
1. **The Dictator’s Misery:** The dictator is proved to be the unhappiest, and the king (moral man) the happiest.
2. **The Three Pleasures:** The three parts of the mind (Intellectual, Passionate, Desirous) each have their own pleasure. The philosopher’s judgment is superior because he has experienced and assessed all three.
3. **True Pleasures:** Intellectual pleasures are "more true and more real" than physical pleasures. Goodness (which the mind tends toward) is more real and more satisfying than things like bread or savories.

The individual is encouraged to use the complex ideal community as a **"paradigm"** to model his or her own inner constitution. This dedication to rational orderliness is the highest goal.

The idea of **detachment** is key here: the philosopher does not deny necessary worldly desires (like food) but chooses "not to find them valuable" as standards of goodness.
