This chapter introduces the "largest and most problematic" wave: the solution to human troubles. The solution is the coincidence of political power and philosophy.

The **Philosopher** is defined as a "lover of knowledge" who seeks **Reality itself** (the eternal, unchanging Forms/Types, like Beauty itself), as opposed to the **"lovers of belief"** who are spectators of the changing visible world. True philosophers possess inherent virtues: honesty (they "won’t willingly give up even minor or worthless parts" of truth), broadness of vision, elegance, courage, and self-discipline.

[[Socrates]] explains the current "disrespect shown to philosophers by society" using the **Ship Analogy**. Society fails to respect the true captain (the philosopher/navigator) because the masses are concerned only with their own desires. The superior nature of the philosopher is also what makes him susceptible to the most profound corruption by the masses and bad education.

This establishes an **elitist morality**. The rule of reason is justified not just by internal psychic health, but by the fact that the philosopher alone has grasped absolute, reliable truth. They must be "compelled to take charge" because they find nothing more valuable than integrity and its rewards.
