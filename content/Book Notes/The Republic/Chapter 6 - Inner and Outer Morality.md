[[Socrates]] locates the four cardinal virtues in the community structure:
1. **Wisdom:** Found in the small class of **Rulers**.
2. **Courage:** Found in the **Auxiliaries** (defined as the ability to retain lawful, educationally inculcated notions about what is to be feared).
3. **Self-Discipline:** The harmony and **agreement** among all three classes that the rational minority should rule the majority.
4. **Morality (Justice):** Is the Principle of Specialization in action—**each class doing its own job** without interfering with the others.

He then uses this framework to identify the **Tripartite Mind**: the **Rational Part** (intelligence/reason), the **Passionate Part** (spirit/courage), and the **Desirous Part**(appetites/money-making).

**Morality** is defined as **"psychic harmony"**, where the three internal components control and are controlled as nature intended. Morality is therefore a state of **"mental health, bloom, and vitality"**; immorality is "mental sickness, deformity, and infirmity".

I think this is the monumental climax. By defining morality as an intrinsic **internal disposition**, Plato successfully defends it against the challenge of Chapter 2. The moral person is happy because their mind is unified and healthy, regardless of external fortunes.
